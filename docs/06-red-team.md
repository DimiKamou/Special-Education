## Strongest case against building at all
The honest case for not building: every durable element of this product is either something a frontier model erodes within 12 months, or something better delivered as a document than as software, and the market that would pay for the rest does not have a budget line.

The generation is already commodity and getting cheaper. The one thing it does badly today — hand you back your own .docx with your header and your Greek fonts intact — is an I/O ergonomics problem, and agentic models with file-editing tools are closing it on a shorter timescale than a part-time solo build. Betting nine months of evenings on a formatting-fidelity wedge is betting against the most reliably improving capability in the stack.

The moat that won this bake-off is a table of professional judgements about which scaffolds contaminate which MYP strands. That artefact is real and valuable — but it is *writing*, not a product. He can publish it in three weekends as a versioned bilingual reference with a ten-page guide: it gets read, cited and argued with by MYP coordinators across thousands of schools, it establishes exactly the authority the software was supposed to monetise, it costs no infrastructure, no DPA, no RLS migration, no Presidio filter, no incorporation, no PI insurance, and it cannot be obsoleted by a model release. If the judgements are good, the document is the moat and the software is an expensive delivery mechanism for it. If the judgements are not good enough to publish under his own name, they are not good enough to sell to a school either.

The ledger business requires humans to enter outcomes, and they will not; that is not a mitigation problem, it is the twenty-year base rate of every IEP-tracking product ever shipped. The institutional business requires an overloaded ΕΔΥ coordinator to author profiles she is not paid to author, for a buyer (the head) whose Greek school has no procurement line, in a national market of perhaps forty plausible customers. The compliance pack, which all three treat as the differentiator, is a cost centre that takes three months and generates a legal claim ('not Article 9') that is wrong.

And the validation loop is closed: he will use it because he wrote it, and his colleagues will try it because he asked them. Nothing in any of the three plans generates a signal that is not contaminated by his own authorship — which means the project can run for nine months looking healthy while telling him nothing.

The opportunity cost is concrete. He already ships a teacher-substitution app with real school-ops customers and an existing distribution path into Greek schools. Adding an access/differentiation module to a product schools already run — where the roster, the auth, the tenancy, the DPA and the relationship already exist — reaches more students in six months than a greenfield platform reaches in two years, and it skips every one of the four fatal objections above.

The only version that survives this argument is the smallest one: a local, single-teacher tool with no server, no student database, no ledger and no institution, built in three weeks of evenings, whose sole purpose is to find out whether span-level review of his own Greek documents is something he still does in week six. If that habit forms, the rest is worth arguing about. If it does not — and the honest prior is that it does not — he has spent three weeks and published a reference document, rather than a year and an ΙΚΕ.

## Objections

### [FATAL] The legality matrix — the winning proposal's entire moat — rests on a false premise about MYP. Construct relevance in MYP is not a property of (subject_group, criterion, strand). MYP strands are deliberately generic, framework-level phrases ('use a wide range of terminology in context'); what is actually assessed is instantiated by the *task-specific clarifications* the teacher writes for that task, plus the statement of inquiry and the ATL focus. IB is non-prescriptive by design. So a global verdict 'word bank is invalidating on I&S Ai' is either trivially coarse (it depends whether the clarification says 'uses terminology unprompted' or 'uses terminology accurately') or simply wrong for half of real tasks. Every MYP coordinator who reads the table will disagree with 10–20% of it, and their disagreement is legitimate. Worse: 400 rows keyed to strand descriptors is a derivative work over IB copyright material distributed to schools, which the proposals never mention. A moat that the buyer's own subject expert can and will overrule, and that may be legally encumbered, is not a moat.

**Fix:** Re-key the unit of legality from (move × subject_group × criterion × strand) to (move × strand × product_type × assessed_verb), and — decisively — make the verdict table a *school-local policy artifact* seeded with ~15 rows, not global IP. The shipped default for an unclassified pair on teaching/formative is `conditional` with a one-line flag; the coordinator's resolution of a `conditional` writes the school's own row, attributed and dated. The cross-tenant product becomes a versioned *verdict pack* (a subscription, like a curriculum publisher's), and the defensible asset becomes 'the school's recorded access-policy decisions, in a form IB evaluation accepts' — which no competitor can copy and no coordinator can overrule, because it is theirs. Never paraphrase IB strand text into the database; store strand *identifiers* plus your own rationale.

### [FATAL] 'No diagnosis field, therefore no Article 9 exposure' is the most load-bearing claim in two of the three proposals and it is wrong. A record reading `initials · el:decode.rate=3 · encoding.spelling=3 · evidence: specialist_report 2025-11-12 · arr: KEDASY:prophoriki_exetasi` is data from which health status is directly inferable, and inferred special-category data is Art. 9 data (recital 35; the CJEU's line in C-184/20 OT on inference). A ΚΕΔΑΣΥ arrangement row is a statutory disability determination with a protocol number. Deleting the word 'dyslexia' changes nothing legally. The first competent DPO says this in minute four of the first meeting, the central defensive sentence collapses in front of the buyer, and every downstream claim ('we never entered the hardest regulatory tier') becomes suspect. The wedge variant is safe only because there is no controller/processor relationship on a laptop — not because the data is not Art. 9.

**Fix:** Change the claim, not the schema. Say, in the DPA and on the first slide: 'This is Article 9 data. We minimise it to a controlled code vocabulary with no narrative and no clinical text; the school processes it under its own Art. 9(2)(g) basis (Ν. 3699/2008, Ν. 4823/2021); it is encrypted at rest in a separate schema on a separate role; no element of it ever enters a model payload; and it is strictly less sensitive than the photocopied γνωμάτευση currently in a folder in the staffroom.' That posture is defensible, costs nothing, and pre-empts the objection instead of being ambushed by it. Say it before the DPO does.

### [FATAL] The ledger — moat's stated business, institutional's entire thesis — depends on humans voluntarily entering outcomes after the lesson, and they will not. Every SEN progress-monitoring and IEP-tracking product in the last twenty years has died precisely here; voluntary post-hoc outcome capture in teacher tools runs in the single-digit percent. And where it does happen, the signal is `teacher_judgement` about an adaptation the teacher themselves chose and accepted — heavily selection-confounded and almost never 'harmful'. So P(worked | move, channel) converges to ~0.85 everywhere, and the `tenant_prior` logistic model over ~300 events per school per term is fitting noise while calling itself a flywheel. Moat's single metric (closed access loops ≥6/student/term) is therefore both the asset and the thing least likely to exist, and it cannot move for two terms, so it steers nothing in weeks 1–12.

**Fix:** Derive the outcome instead of asking for it. The only outcome signal with a real capture rate is behavioural: (a) *reapplication* — the same move applied to the same student on a later document without edit; (b) *edit distance on reapplication*; (c) *de facto fade* — the move stops being reapplied. Instrument those three from day one; they are free and unfakeable. Demote human outcome capture to exactly one prompted item per week, chosen by the fade-review queue, one tap, three options. Delete the learned prior from v1 entirely and use outcome data only to break scorer ties after n≥500 events. Replace the single metric with one that moves weekly: *unedited reapplications per week*, with a kill rule at <2 for three consecutive weeks after week 8.

### [FATAL] Per-item criterion/strand tagging is the hidden adoption tax and nobody priced it. It is listed as 'teacher confirms in one pass, cached by content hash', but it is the hard precondition for the legality filter, the strand-union volume rule, and the answer-leak gate. A 12-item I&S worksheet is 3–5 minutes of genuine cognitive work per document, every document, done at 23:10 by the person the tool exists to save time for. Against a stopwatched manual baseline of 25–35 minutes, tagging plus mode declaration plus diff review plausibly consumes the entire claimed saving on exactly the documents (dense, multi-item, summative-adjacent) where the tool is supposed to be worth most.

**Fix:** Make per-item strand tagging required only when mode = summative_internal. For teaching and formative, the teacher tags the *document* with criteria in three clicks and volume-reduction moves are simply disabled (the refusal reads: 'I can't cut items without knowing which strand each one evidences — tag them, or I'll keep everything'). Auto-propose per-item tags from command term + item text + marks and require confirmation only on items below a confidence threshold; target ≤4 confirmations on a 12-item sheet. Cache by content_hash so a reused source is tagged once ever.

### [SERIOUS] 'Verification is O(changes)' — the wedge's core bet and the adoption argument all three inherit — is false exactly where the product is most valuable. On a dense 400-word ΙΤΥΕ Greek source, S-SPLIT-SENTENCE + S-DENOMINALISE-EL + S-GENITIVE-UNWIND-EL + S-RESOLVE-ANAPHORA touch nearly every sentence: that is 25–40 change cards, not six. And Greek register drift (λόγιο→καθομιλουμένη mismatch, wrong connective, anaphora resolved to the wrong referent) is only detectable *in context*, so a philologist re-reads the whole paragraph anyway. O(changes) collapses to O(document) precisely on the dense Greek text that justified building a Greek analyser at all.

**Fix:** Make the default mode *additive*, not rewriting: gloss-in-place, colometry, organiser, discourse stems, command-term unpack, answer box, known-lexicon highlight — all verifiable at a glance because the original text is untouched. Rewriting transforms go behind an explicit per-block 'rewrite this paragraph' action the teacher invokes, and are hard-capped at ≤12 accepted rewrite ops per artifact. Additionally, render rewrite ops as a side-by-side paragraph pair, not as inline change cards, because the unit of judgement for a rewritten Greek paragraph is the paragraph.

### [SERIOUS] The 'access routes' architecture — cluster students by barrier vector, assign each to a route, record it in a per-student ledger — is the best available candidate for an AI Act Annex III 3(b) finding ('evaluate learning outcomes, including when used to steer the learning process'). All three proposals assume they are outside Annex III and cite that assumption as a design win. But a system that measures a named student's functional profile and determines which version of the material that student receives, then records it, is literally steering, and the product's own sales pitch says so. The Art. 6(4) memo the proposals file is a self-assessment, not a shield; the HDPA as market-surveillance authority is not bound by it.

**Fix:** Move the student→route assignment out of the system. Code proposes clusters; the teacher drags chips (that UI already exists in all three); the system never writes an automatic student→route mapping, and the ledger records 'teacher assigned X to route B' as a human act with a named actor. Make the unit of generated output the *class artifact set*, not the student. That is a one-line change at step 8 and it is the difference between 'content adaptation with human assignment' and 'automated steering'. File the Art. 6(4) memo *and* register under Art. 49(2) anyway, and state the residual risk in the memo rather than asserting the conclusion.

### [SERIOUS] The compliance artefact pack is priced at 'a week' and is three months. EN 301 549 conformance for a ProseMirror diff surface with colour-coded margin markers, custom decorations, overlay focus management and keyboard-only op navigation requires a real audit and will fail first pass (colour as sole channel on barrier families alone). A 'pre-filled DPIA' handed to a school is a category error — the DPIA is the *controller's* risk assessment; a DPO who signs yours has not done one, and a good one will refuse. The bilingual AI-literacy module with per-teacher completion records makes you a processor of *staff* data with its own RoPA, retention schedule and lawful basis. RoPA 'for both parties' is not a thing you can author for them either.

**Fix:** Ship exactly three documents in v1: (1) an Art. 28 DPA with sub-processor list at a stable URL, zero-retention and no-training terms; (2) a 6-page document titled 'DPIA input pack' that explicitly says it is input to the school's DPIA, structured as answers to the school's questions (data categories, flows, recipients, region, retention, risks-and-mitigations); (3) the Art. 6(4) classification memo. Defer the AI-literacy module, the child-readable notice, the model card and any conformance *statement* to school #3. Treat accessibility as a design constraint from day one (never colour alone, real focus order, visible focus ring, aria-live on op decisions) and make no conformance claim until someone audits it.

### [SERIOUS] 'No evidence, no move' is elegant, will be loved by an inclusion lead in a demo, and is a growth cap plus security theatre in practice. It is trivially gamed (the teacher types a date), it withholds output on exactly the undiagnosed majority the product claims to serve, and it guarantees that the first real use for a real student produces *fewer* moves than pasting into a chatbot. It buys ritual, not validity.

**Fix:** Move the gate from generation to permission. Unevidenced barriers still generate moves, watermarked `provisional`, which (a) cannot write an AccessEvent, (b) cannot appear in any export pack, (c) cannot touch summative_internal, (d) cannot authorise an arrangement. The UI offers one-click 'record this observation' at the point of acceptance — which is when the teacher has just seen evidence of the barrier in their own document, the highest-yield moment there will ever be. Evidence capture becomes a by-product of use rather than a precondition for it.

### [ANNOYING] The controlled vocabulary is repeatedly called an asset and it is not one. It is a week of work, it is fully visible in the product's own UI, and the three proposals produced 20, 24 and 31 codes with incompatible boundaries — which is itself evidence that the boundaries are arbitrary. Arbitrary SEN taxonomies are the classic thing the first educational psychologist in the room contests. Worse, nobody tested whether the codes are *discriminative*: if two codes never cause different moves to be selected, they are two names for one row.

**Fix:** Run the discrimination test on day 3: for every pair of codes, does any card in the library select on one and not the other? Merge every pair that fails. My bet is 31 collapses to 12–15. Anchor each survivor to an ICF-CY code and to one teacher-answerable observable question ('loses the thread after about three sentences'), and stop describing the ontology as IP — say out loud that it is a join key, and that the asset is the verdicts and the ledger.

### [SERIOUS] Nothing in any of the three detects the *false refusal*. Every validator, gate and badge is oriented at catching over-simplification. There is no instrument at all for 'a move that was legal was withheld', which is invisible by construction, uncorrectable, and is what makes fail-closed slowly render the tool useless for the hardest students — the ones where the legal move set is smallest. Combined with 15 seeded verdict rows and a fail-closed default, month one reads as 'the tool refused most of what I asked', which is the second-use cliff wearing a compliance badge.

**Fix:** Every refusal renders with an override control that writes a verdict-*candidate* row carrying the teacher's typed reason, the task, the strand and the timestamp. The refusal log becomes the authoring queue for the matrix — that is how 15 rows becomes 400 without a curation sprint, and it is the only mechanism in any of the three that scales verdict authorship past one person's bus factor. Track `refusal rate per artifact` as a first-class product alarm: above ~20% on teaching/formative the matrix is mis-seeded, not the teacher wrong.

### [SERIOUS] The docx round-trip premise test is right but under-specified and will fail on real Greek worksheets more often than any proposal admits. python-docx does not handle text boxes (w:txbxContent — extremely common for answer spaces in Word-authored Greek worksheets), OMML equations, SDT content controls, SmartArt, or shapes; and the (body, paragraph, run) anchor assumes run stability, while Word routinely splits a single sentence into 5–8 runs on rsid, proofing state and w:lang boundaries, so an in-run text replacement silently destroys formatting boundaries. 'Untouched blocks byte-identical' is also stated wrongly in all three — python-docx rewrites the package; bytes will differ everywhere.

**Fix:** Restate the test as XML-canonical equality of untouched `<w:p>` elements after normalising rsid/proofing attributes, run it on 20 of his real documents (not 3), and ship a named-refusal list in week 1: 'this document contains text boxes / equations / content controls — I can only edit the main body; paste the rest or accept body-only edits.' Write edits at paragraph level by rebuilding the run sequence from a captured formatting template rather than mutating a single run in place.

### [FATAL] Pricing and TAM do not survive contact with Greece. €1,200–8,000 per school, sold to an inclusion lead with no budget authority, in a country where state schools have no procurement line for this at all. The realistic buyer set is 30–50 private/international IB schools nationally. Total dominance is a sub-€500k/yr business, reached in year four, built by one person with a full teaching timetable. Meanwhile the three plans' 12-week schedules, discounted at the same rate the judges already applied to wedge ('read day 9 as week 3'), land v1 around June 2027 — by which time frontier models with file-editing tools have eaten the formatting-fidelity wedge.

**Fix:** Choose the market in month 1, because the two choices imply different products. Either (a) the global IB world-school play — 5,700+ schools, English-first, MYP/DP packs only, no ΚΕΔΑΣΥ modelling, no Greek national curriculum, Greek as an unusual-strength differentiator and IMS as pilot site not market; or (b) the individual-teacher tool at €12–18/month EU-wide with no institution in the loop, no ledger, no DPA, where the Greek analyser is the defensible bit. Do not build the Greek-institutional version: it has the highest build cost, the slowest sale, the weakest buyer and the smallest market of the three options on the table.

### [ANNOYING] The fidelity badge and the refusals panel are adoption theatre for the teacher, even though the refusals panel is the single best sales artifact for a coordinator. '11/11' is a gauge that always reads full, because failures block before render. A gauge that never moves stops being seen in week two. And a panel enumerating what the tool declined to do is exactly what a person at 23:10 does not want on the screen where they are trying to finish.

**Fix:** Show the badge only when it has news: '2 ops dropped — command term would have downgraded on Q4', with a link to the span. Move the refusals list off the working screen and onto the teacher sheet and the export, where the coordinator reads it and the teacher does not have to.

### [SERIOUS] The Greek band model trained on ΙΤΥΕ Διόφαντος, gated at Spearman ρ ≥ 0.7 against the builder's ranking of 30 passages, is pseudo-rigour in two of the three v1 scopes. Grade-labelled textbook corpora confound topic, genre and typography with grade, so the model learns topic. ρ estimated from n=30 has roughly a ±0.2–0.25 confidence interval, so 'ρ ≥ 0.7' is a coin flip with a lab coat. And validating an objectivity claim against one person's ranking makes the metric a measure of agreement with Dimitris, not of difficulty. The corpus licensing (ΙΤΥΕ/ΙΕΠ public materials) is also unexamined in all three.

**Fix:** Drop the band model from v1 (the wedge is right). Report the three driving features with the offending span quoted — that is more actionable than a band and honest about what is known. If the model ships in month 4+, validate on ≥300 *pairwise* comparisons collected from ≥3 φιλόλογοι, report inter-rater agreement first and model-vs-consensus second, and never display a number the analyser cannot defend against a colleague.

### [SERIOUS] The hard constraint 'MYP criteria and level descriptors are immutable' — inherited by all three and enforced as a blocking gate — is not actually true, and an inclusion lead will know it. IB permits schools to modify MYP assessment criteria for a student with documented needs in defined circumstances, with documented consequences (including eligibility effects for MYP course results/eAssessment). A product that hard-refuses a legitimate, IB-sanctioned institutional practice looks naive to the one person whose endorsement it needs.

**Fix:** Add a third classification alongside `access` and `modification`: an explicit `criteria_modification` path that is refused to teachers, available only to inclusion_lead + programme_coordinator jointly, requires a typed rationale, prints the eligibility consequence on the artifact, and writes a distinctly-typed ledger row. Refusing it entirely is wrong; allowing it silently is worse; naming it and gating it is the thing that makes a coordinator trust the product.

### [SERIOUS] Validation of the whole system is one teacher at one school who also wrote it. He will use it because he built it; his colleagues at IMS will try it because he is their colleague. Neither fact is evidence. All three plans treat 'Dimitris uses it weekly' and '3–4 IMS colleagues touch it' as the adoption proof points, and both are structurally false positives.

**Fix:** Define one external falsification test and schedule it for week 8: hand the laptop-local build to two teachers at a *different* school who owe him nothing, with no demo, no onboarding and no support, and measure whether either exports a printed pack in week 2 unprompted. If neither does, the wedge premise is wrong and no amount of ledger or matrix work fixes it. Write the test and its kill condition into the README before writing any application code.

## What the synthesis had to change

- Stop claiming the system is outside Article 9. Adopt the honest posture instead — Art. 9 data, minimised to codes, no narrative, school's own Art. 9(2)(g) basis, never in the model payload, encrypted in a separate schema, strictly less exposing than the photocopy in the staffroom — and put it in the DPA and on the first slide before any DPO raises it.
- Re-found the legality matrix as a school-local, coordinator-authored policy artefact seeded at ~15 rows, not 400 rows of global IP. Ship `conditional` (not `invalidating`) as the default for unclassified pairs on teaching and formative; fail closed only on summative_internal. Never store paraphrased IB strand text — store strand identifiers plus your own rationale.
- Make every refusal write a verdict-candidate row with the teacher's typed override reason. The refusal log is the matrix's authoring queue; that is the only mechanism that scales verdict authorship past one person and the only instrument that detects false refusals. Track refusal rate per artifact as a product alarm with a ~20% ceiling on non-summative.
- Move the evidence gate from generation to permission. Unevidenced barriers still emit moves, watermarked `provisional`, barred from AccessEvents, export packs, summative mode and arrangement authorisation. Capture the observation at the moment of acceptance, not before generation.
- Stop asking humans for outcomes as the primary signal. Derive outcome from reapplication, edit distance on reapplication, and de facto fade; prompt for exactly one human outcome per week from the fade queue. Delete the learned tenant prior from v1 and use outcome data only to break scorer ties above n≈500 events.
- Replace the single metric with one that produces a weekly signal in weeks 1–12: unedited reapplications per week (same move, same student-set, later document, accepted without edit). Keep a written kill rule: below 2 for three consecutive weeks after week 8, stop. Moat's closed-loop metric and institutional's PCR-3/14 are renewal metrics, not steering metrics — demote both to secondary.
- Make the default transform mode additive (gloss, colometry, organiser, stems, command-term unpack, layout) and put rewriting behind an explicit per-block action, hard-capped at ≤12 rewrite ops per artifact, rendered as side-by-side paragraph pairs. The O(changes) verification claim only holds for additive edits.
- Require per-item strand tagging only on summative_internal; for teaching and formative, tag the document with criteria in three clicks and disable volume reduction with a stated reason. Auto-propose item tags and require confirmation on ≤4 items per sheet.
- Take automatic student→route assignment out of the system: code proposes clusters, the teacher assigns chips, the ledger records a named human act. The unit of generated output is the class artifact set, not the student. This is the single cheapest move that keeps the product out of an Annex III 3(b) steering argument.
- Cut the v1 compliance pack to three documents — Art. 28 DPA with sub-processor URL, a 6-page 'DPIA input pack' explicitly labelled as input to the school's DPIA, and the Art. 6(4) memo. Defer the AI-literacy module, child notice, model card and any EN 301 549 conformance statement. Treat accessibility as a build constraint (never colour alone, real focus order, aria-live) and make no conformance claim until audited.
- Drop the ΙΤΥΕ-calibrated Greek band model from v1; report the three driving features with the offending span quoted. If it ships later, validate on ≥300 pairwise judgements from ≥3 philologists and report inter-rater agreement before model agreement — never Spearman against one person's ranking of 30 passages.
- Restate the docx round-trip test as XML-canonical equality of untouched w:p elements after normalising rsid and proofing attributes, run it on 20 real documents, write edits by rebuilding the run sequence from a captured formatting template, and ship a named-refusal list for text boxes, equations, content controls and SmartArt in week 1.
- Add an explicit `criteria_modification` path, gated to inclusion_lead + programme_coordinator jointly, with typed rationale and the eligibility consequence printed on the artifact. Hard-refusing all criteria modification is factually wrong about IB practice and will cost credibility with the one person whose endorsement matters.
- Run the ontology discrimination test on day 3 — merge every code pair no strategy card distinguishes — and stop calling the vocabulary an asset. Expect 24–31 codes to collapse to 12–15.
- Pick the market in month 1: global IB world schools (English-first, MYP/DP packs, no ΚΕΔΑΣΥ modelling, Greece as pilot not market) or individual teachers at €12–18/month with no institution in the loop. Do not build the Greek-institutional version — highest build cost, slowest sale, weakest buyer, smallest market.
- Schedule one external falsification test for week 8: two teachers at a different school, no demo, no onboarding, no support; measure whether either exports a printed pack in week 2 unprompted. Write it and its kill condition into the README before writing application code, because Dimitris's own usage and his IMS colleagues' usage are structurally false positives.
- Show the fidelity badge only when it has news (ops dropped, repairs made, with the span linked), and move the refused-moves panel off the teacher's working screen onto the teacher sheet and the export where the coordinator reads it.
