# Subject playbooks

Three text-heavy humanities subjects. No maths, no science diagrams, no lab protocols —
which is good news: the product is a **text-access engine**, narrow and deep, and every
subject in scope shares one substrate.

Legend: **✅ access** (safe on summative) · **⚠️ conditional** (depends on the assessed
strand) · **⛔ construct contamination** (invalidates).

---

## History — MYP I&S / Ιστορία Γυμνασίου

The assessed construct is **causation, significance, source evaluation, interpretation**.
It is almost never reading fluency. That asymmetry is the most generous in the curriculum:
the source text can be worked on hard, because the text is not the object of assessment.

| Move | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| De-nominalise the source («Η διά της βίας επιβολή του καθεστώτος» → «Το καθεστώς επιβλήθηκε με βία») | ✅ | Preserves every proposition. Greek history textbooks are pathologically nominalised; this is the highest-yield single transform in the subject. |
| Unwind genitive chains, resolve pronouns to referents | ✅ | Syntactic de-densification. Propositions unchanged. |
| Gloss tier-3 terms in apposition (keep the term, add the explanation) | ✅ | Preserves Criterion A. |
| **Substitute** tier-3 terms | ⛔ on A | The term *is* the assessed knowledge. |
| Actor / agent card (who is who, one line each) | ✅ | History prose assumes named-entity knowledge students do not have. Cheapest real win in the subject. |
| Timeline strip along the top of the source | ✅ | Chronology is background, not construct — unless the task assesses sequencing. |
| Blank OPVL / provenance grid | ✅ | Layout aid. |
| Pre-filled OPVL, or any grid that names the limitation | ⛔ on D | This is *the* refusal. Print the reason on the teacher copy. |
| Causal connective bank («Ως συνέπεια…», «Ωστόσο…») | ⚠️ | Pure access when the task assesses A. Contaminating when the task assesses causal reasoning under D — the connective *is* the reasoning. |
| Writing frame with paragraph structure | ⚠️ | Contaminates C. Fine when only A and D are assessed. |
| "What changed / what stayed the same" organiser | ✅ | Structures thinking without supplying content. |
| Reduce number of sources | ⚠️ | Legal only if the remaining sources still evidence every assessed strand. Requires per-item tagging — so restrict to summative mode. |

**Greek-specific:** ΙΤΥΕ history textbook prose runs to genitive depth 4 and 7+
nominalisations per 100 words routinely. Measure it, quote the offending span, and let the
teacher see why the passage is hard. Most will not have noticed it is the syntax, not the
content.

## Literature — MYP Language & Literature / Λογοτεχνία

**The primary text is the object of assessment. It is never rewritten, simplified,
modernised, or paraphrased in place. Not once, not ever.** Hard-coded refusal, no override,
no coordinator gate. This is the only absolute in the system.

Everything moves to the **margin** or to the **task**.

| Move | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Marginal gloss (word / phrase, alongside, text untouched) | ✅ | The scholarly apparatus has done this for two thousand years. |
| Colometry — one κῶλον or one verse per line | ✅ | Honours the text's own structure. Index-based splits only: never retype a character. |
| Known-lexicon highlighting | ✅ | Shows the student how much they already hold. |
| Paraphrase printed **alongside**, clearly marked as not the text | ⚠️ | Access on a task assessing theme. Contaminating when the task assesses *language and style*, which in L&L is most of Criterion A. |
| Rewriting the poem / passage | ⛔ always | Destroys the object. |
| Supplying an interpretation, a theme statement, or "what this is about" | ⛔ on A | The interpretation is the entire assessment. |
| Unpacking the command term of the *question* | ✅ | The question is not the text. |
| "Notice → name → why" response ladder | ✅ | Structures the move from observation to analysis without supplying either. |
| Writing frame for the response | ⚠️ | Contaminates B. |
| Spellcheck / word bank on the student's writing | ⛔ on D | D *is* language use. Same tool, pure access in History. |
| Audio of the text | ✅ | Decoding is not the construct; interpretation is. Under-used and free. |

**Greek-specific:** Σολωμός, Παπαδιαμάντης, καθαρεύουσα residue, dialect, archaic
morphology. A gloss line under the verse beats any rewrite, and preserves the thing that
makes the text worth teaching. For Ancient Greek: colometry, interlinear morphological
gloss giving **form, not meaning**, from a real analyser — and the model never generates a
character of Ancient Greek. One wrong περισπωμένη in front of a φιλόλογος ends the
product's credibility permanently.

## Modern Greek language — Νεοελληνική Γλώσσα

The hardest case, because **the construct is the language itself**. Most of the access
moves that are free in History are contaminating here.

| Move | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Simplify a *stimulus* text the student reads to then write about | ✅ | The stimulus is input, not the assessed object. |
| Simplify the text when the task assesses comprehension of *that* text | ⛔ | That is the construct. |
| Vocabulary bank for the student's own production | ⛔ | Lexical range is assessed. |
| Register table (λόγιο ↔ καθομιλουμένη, with examples) taught *before* the task | ✅ | Instruction, not scaffold. Type it `atl_taught` with a fade date. |
| Worked example + fading sequence | ✅ | The single best-evidenced move for this subject. |
| Model text with the structure annotated | ✅ | Annotating structure ≠ supplying it. |
| Orthographic support | ⚠️ | Pure access when the task assesses argument. Contaminating when orthography is assessed — and in Γυμνάσιο it usually is, in part. |
| Extra time | ✅ | Rate is not the construct, unless the task is explicitly timed production. |

**Greek orthography, precisely:** the dyslexia signature is *rate* plus *inflectional-ending
spelling* (-ει / -η / -οι, ο vs ω, ι / η / υ / ει / οι), never reading accuracy — Greek is
shallow for reading and deep for spelling. A tool that screens for accuracy finds nothing
and reports a false negative on a genuinely dyslexic Greek student.

**Never answer a writing barrier with more grammar drill.** Graham & Perin 2007 puts
isolated grammar instruction at d ≈ −0.32 on writing quality. Hard-code it
`contraindicated` and show the citation when a teacher asks for it, because they will.

## Greek as a second language (the quiet majority)

In a Greek international school this cohort is routinely larger than the SEN cohort and
gets almost nothing. It is a **different barrier profile**, not a milder one:

- Needs **morphological** support — case endings, verb aspect, article agreement — not
  decoding support.
- Needs **register** support: textbook Greek is far from spoken Greek.
- Does **not** need chunking, spacing, or reduced text volume, and giving it is
  patronising and useless.
- Bilingual glossing (EL term → EN gloss, term kept) is the highest-yield move and is
  ✅ everywhere except Language Acquisition Reading tasks.

If this cohort is the larger one in your rooms, it should drive v1 and the product is
called something other than a special-education tool.

## The shared substrate

Every move above reduces to seven operations on a document, which is why one engine covers
three subjects:

1. **Gloss in place** — add, never replace
2. **Colometry / segmentation** — index-based splits, characters never retyped
3. **Syntactic de-densification** — split clauses, resolve pronouns, de-nominalise, de-passivise
4. **Organiser insertion** — blank grids, timelines, actor cards, comparison tables
5. **Command-term unpack** — what the question is actually asking for
6. **Discourse stems** — structure without content
7. **Render directives** — spacing, line length, font size, one-question-per-page

Ten of these cost zero model tokens. Ship those first: they are always correct, always
specific, and they carry the habit while the generative path is still shallow.
