# Alternatives considered

Three independent proposals were designed and scored on three criteria.

| direction | total | shipability | pedagogy | durability |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## [wedge] Δίοδος / Diodos

Drop your own .docx in; get your own .docx back — same header, same logo, same tables — with tracked, reason-tagged changes in three access tiers, and a mechanical proof that the MYP command term and criterion survived.

**Core bet.** The bottleneck was never generation quality — a chatbot already writes a decent simplification. The bottleneck is verification cost: a regenerated document forces O(document) re-reading every single time, so the net time saving is negative and the tool dies in week three. Diodos bets everything on emitting typed, span-anchored edit operations against the teacher's immutable file, so verification drops to O(changes) — and on a single 20-code vocabulary shared by the learner line and a deterministic text analyser, so every move fires only where a measured demand in *this* paragraph collides with an evidenced barrier. The corollary bet is subtractive: deleting the student database, the diagnosis field, the school, and the server is what makes it shippable in nine days and simultaneously dissolves the Article 9 problem by construction rather than by policy.

**Biggest risk.** The second-use cliff caused by a shallow analyser. Week 1 the output is clever; week 3 it has converged on the same five moves regardless of the document, he stops opening it, and nobody says why — the postmortem misattributes it to 'teachers resist technology'. The detector is cheap and must run from week 2: a content-ablation test (same profile, five different texts — strategy-set Jaccard must be < 0.5) and a profile-ablation test (same text, five archetype profiles — Jaccard < 0.45), promoted to blocking CI gates in month 2. The mitigations are structural rather than prompt-level: every change card must quote the span of HIS text it touched, so genericness is visible in one second rather than after three weeks; the Greek triad (genitive chains, nominalisation density, learned participles) must be measured per block rather than per document, so the moves land on specific sentences; and the ten free render/deterministic cards must ship first, because they are always specific and always correct and they carry the habit while the generative side is still shallow. The acute two-week risk is different and is settled on day one: if python-docx cannot round-trip his three messiest real worksheets byte-identically under a no-op apply, the entire premise — 'it gives you back your own file' — is false, and that test is the first thing written.

## [moat] Prosvasi

A construct-safe differentiation engine built on a frozen barrier/demand ontology, a hand-curated evidence-linked move library with a per-strand legality matrix, and a longitudinal access ledger — whose by-product is the exact evidence pack an IB evaluation visit and a Greek ΕΔΥ review both demand and no school currently has.

**Core bet.** The transformation is a commodity — a frontier chatbot does it well today and will do it better next year. The defensible asset is the three-way join nobody owns: one controlled vocabulary shared by the learner model and the content analyser, a hand-authored (move × subject_group × criterion × strand) legality matrix that says which scaffold is access and which is construct contamination, and a per-student ledger of what was applied, faded, reviewed, and what actually happened. Bet that schools buy the record, not the rewrite, and that the record is what a general model can never accumulate.

**Biggest risk.** The curation bottleneck is one person, and it is the moat and the single point of failure simultaneously. The legality matrix and the move library are hand-authored professional judgements that only Dimitris can make for Individuals & Societies, Language & Literature and Νεοελληνικά — and because the matrix fails closed, every subject he has not personally encoded refuses almost everything. The tool therefore risks being excellent in the humanities corridor and inert everywhere else, which caps it at one department in one school and makes the ledger too thin to be worth exporting. The compounding version of the risk is that asset-first sequencing puts twelve weeks between the first commit and the fourth teacher, so he could spend a quarter authoring 400 verdict rows before discovering whether colleagues will accept span-level review at all. Mitigations, in order: run the naive single-prompt baseline on day 2 of week 1 so the quality delta is a measured number before any curation cost is sunk; get the product into his own daily lesson prep by end of week 3 and colleagues' hands by week 8, not month 3; make Sciences and Maths visibly 'not yet encoded' rather than silently broken; and build the SENCo-facing library-authoring UI in week 12 specifically so verdict authorship can leave his laptop before it becomes a bus factor.

## [institutional] Prosvasi

The inclusion lead owns the barrier profile, every subject teacher's worksheet inherits it, and every accepted change writes the evidence record that IB programme evaluation and Greek ΕΔΥ review already demand and no school can currently produce.

**Core bet.** The differentiated worksheet is bait; the auditable Access Record is the asset. We bet a school pays 10–20× a teacher-productivity price for a system that converts ordinary Tuesday-afternoon differentiation into IB inclusive-access evidence, ΕΠΕ/ΕΔΥ records and ATL fade documentation as a by-product — and that the only way to get that record is to make the inclusion lead the sole author of the profile and every subject teacher a consumer of it, enforced in the permission model rather than in a policy document.

**Biggest risk.** The inclusion lead is a single-point bottleneck and she is not paid to be a data-entry clerk. Institution-first routes the entire critical path through one overloaded person whose incentive is compliance rather than adoption; if she authors six profiles in September and stops, PCR-3/14 is structurally capped, the Access Record is empty, the export packs have nothing to export, and the product silently degrades to Quick Mode — i.e. to a commodity text leveller competing with MagicSchool at $8/month, where we lose. Three mitigations, all in v1: profile authoring is time-boxed to ~90 seconds per student by the 10-dimension structured intake with typed evidence quotes rather than document upload; the €800–1,500 onboarding is not training but a day of co-authoring her first 40 profiles with her; and the teacher observation inbox plus the Quick Mode conversion ratchet shift her ongoing job from authoring to curating, which is the only version of the job that survives a real term. The tripwire is measured weekly from week 1 of the pilot, not at renewal: if fewer than 25 profiles exist by day 30, or PCR-3/14 is under 25% at day 90, the deployment is failing for this reason and no amount of generation quality will fix it.

# Judgments

## Criterion: shipability — winner: wedge

Not close. Diodos is the only one of the three that a single part-time teacher-engineer can actually have in front of a Year 4 class this term, because it is the only one whose week 1 produces working output rather than YAML, a Postgres tenancy model, or a calibrated regression on a national textbook corpus. It deletes the server, the accounts, the student database and the diagnosis field - and each deletion removes both engineering weeks and an institutional approval gate at the same time. Its ten zero-LLM render cards mean the app is useful before the generative path works at all, which is the only credible answer to the second-use cliff. Its day-1 docx no-op round-trip test is the single best decision in any of the three documents: it tries to kill the premise before the premise costs anything. The honest deduction is that its day counts assume a person who is not simultaneously teaching a full timetable; read 'day 9' as 'week 3' and the plan still holds, because every day is independently falsifiable and the order is right. Moat loses on its own thesis - it explicitly sequences a 31-code ontology, a 400-row hand-authored legality matrix and an ITYE-trained ordinal model ahead of the app, which is the exact corpus-first dependency this criterion exists to punish, and its week-2 research task is mispriced by a factor of four. Institutional loses hardest and most avoidably: it makes the SENCo the load-bearing element of a product built by someone with no authority over her calendar, spends week 1 on RLS policies, and defers first real use behind 60 strategy cards, a seeded legality matrix and an EL/EN compliance pack for a product with no customer. The final design should be Diodos's build sequence with Institutional's Quick Mode as the permanent no-roster front door, and Moat's fail-closed rule so the legality matrix can start at fifteen rows instead of four hundred.

## Criterion: pedagogy — winner: moat

Moat wins because it is the only proposal that resolves access-vs-content at the strand, which is the only level at which that distinction is actually true in MYP. Spellcheck is construct-irrelevant in Lang&Lit Criterion A and construct-destroying in D; a blank OPVL grid is legal on I&S Diii and a pre-filled one is not. No diagnosis, no document, and no prompt can tell you that — only a hand-authored (move × criterion × strand) verdict can, and only moat and institutional built one. Wedge's flat construct-token list proves its own weakness in its own showcase copy: it refuses the blank OPVL grid on Diii, which is a correct-looking refusal that is pedagogically wrong and would train the teacher to distrust the filter. On label≠need, wedge's deletion is the cleanest but also the most evasive — the γνωμάτευση is the only document that exists in a Greek school, and moat's treatment of it (stored probabilistic prior, rendered 'assumed, not observed', inert until human confirmation, observed permanently outranks assumed) is the harder and better answer. Institutional's label refusal is the sharpest single sentence in the whole field and its 7–8 reachability gate is the best MYP-native anti-ceiling check anywhere, but it grades evidence per card rather than per (card × barrier), defers the numeracy channels an I&S teacher needs in term one, and its metric never asks whether anything worked. That last point is decisive: moat is the only design that closes the loop — outcome captured by a human, fade stepped on a Sweller/Renkl schedule, demand_restore tracked, and a ceiling audit that fires when a teacher's operations only ever reduce demand. Everything else is a generator with good manners. Graft wedge's ablation gates and institutional's 7–8 reachability validator and observation-cannot-authorise-an-arrangement rule into moat, and drop the fixed k=3 defence to a genuine gap statement.

## Criterion: durability — winner: moat

All three share roughly 70% of the same spine — one vocabulary on both sides of the join, span-anchored SpanEdits under strict schema, deterministic validators, the Greek morphosyntactic analyser, Annex III refusals, EU inference. So this criterion discriminates almost entirely on what accretes after week four, and there the three separate cleanly. Wedge accretes nothing: it deletes the store, the institution and the second user on purpose, so at month 18 it is a very good Greek measurement instrument plus a docx diff — and the docx diff is the part an agentic frontier model with file tools eats first. Its own month-3 plan names the ledger as the real product, which is a concession, not a roadmap. Institutional builds the deepest lock-in and the thinnest flywheel: its record makes leaving expensive but never makes the next generation better, and it routes the entire asset through one ΕΔΥ coordinator who is not paid to be a data-entry clerk — its own failure mode is explicitly 'degrade to MagicSchool.' Moat wins because it owns the only artefact in the set that appreciates as models improve: a hand-authored, fail-closed, human-attested construct legality matrix, whose value is authority rather than content, plus the only genuine outcome→prior→selector loop and a single metric that is literally a compounding metric. Its weaknesses are real and should be fixed rather than traded away: per-tenant-only compounding caps the network effect, and a one-person curation bottleneck caps subject coverage — pull the institutional proposal's Quick Mode ratchet and role model forward to month 2 and put the wedge's ablation gates in CI from week 5, and the bus factor stops being terminal.
